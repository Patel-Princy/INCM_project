## 1. Executive summary

**The recordings differ, and the difference is measurable. It is not in the
stimulus, not in the resting membrane, and not in passive membrane kinetics.**

The question this phase was given is narrow: the same specimen produced **12
spikes** during a +170 pA, 1000 ms step in the COARSE block (sweep 38) and
**exactly 1 spike** in each of four FINEST repeats (sweeps 49–52), on command
arrays that are **bit-identical** over the 1 204 001-sample common window. The
Phase 2E perisomatic model returns 12 spikes for that command. This phase
investigates the recordings. No model was executed, refitted, recalibrated or
reduced; nothing was written to the NWB.

**What the recordings establish (OBSERVED).**

*The stimulus did nothing different.* Sweep 38's command and sweeps 49–52's
commands differ in **0 samples**, with `max |Δ| = 0.000e+00 pA` over 1 204 001
samples. The epoch agrees at onset 1.0200 s and duration 1000.000 ms; the
sampling rate (200 kHz), amplifier gain (0.0100), access resistance (12.23 MΩ)
and seal (2.336) are identical for all 44 long-square sweeps. The two blocks
merely *reach* the same current by different multiplication paths — COARSE
applies 1.0× to a 170 pA template, FINEST applies 1.7× to a 100 pA template — and
the arithmetic lands on identical samples.

*The resting membrane did nothing different.* Pre-step baseline median is
−80.812 mV in sweep 38 and −81.125 / −80.656 / −80.812 / −81.156 mV in sweeps
49–52: an offset of **+0.125 mV** with per-sweep SDs of 0.066–0.118 mV. Passive
response to the same current is also unchanged: the single-exponential charging
time constant is **31.35 ms** in COARSE against **28.76–32.53 ms** in FINEST, and
the relaxation after step offset is **17.57 ms** against **16.94–17.57 ms**.

*The sustained level differs slightly, and in the wrong direction for a simple
"less excitable" story.* The steady-state median over the last 200 ms of the step
is **−47.094 mV** in COARSE and **−46.266 mV** (mean of four) in FINEST. FINEST
sits **0.828 mV more depolarised**, not less — and still fires once.

*The difference is localised to post-spike recovery.* After its first spike,
sweep 38 climbs **+6.594 mV above its own sustained plateau** within 35.2 ms (the
window is truncated by the next spike) and fires again; across the full train its
11 intervals all overshoot the plateau by **+10.656 to +14.719 mV** (median
+13.344). The four FINEST sweeps climb only **+1.953 mV on average** (range
+1.594 to +2.750) and then sit flat for **~900 ms**, never re-reaching threshold.
The FINEST measurement window is roughly 25× longer than COARSE's, so the
comparison is biased *in FINEST's favour*: given more time, it still did not
fire.

*The COARSE train changes progressively within the step.* Inter-spike interval
lengthens from **39.25 ms to 85.935 ms** (Spearman ρ = 0.773, permutation
p = 0.0039 in the observed direction); instantaneous rate falls 25.48 → 11.64 Hz;
the threshold rises from **−41.188 mV to −32.500 mV** (ρ = +0.811, p = 0.00135);
spike amplitude falls from **96.313 mV to 76.813 mV** (ρ = −0.811, p = 0.0012);
maximum upstroke velocity falls from **306.25 to 250 V/s** (ρ = −0.706,
p = 0.0056). Spike width does **not** change reliably (trough reference ρ = 0.354,
p = 0.128; the local-median reference gives p = 0.046, and the two references
disagree — reported as equivocal, not as a finding), and AHP depth is flat
(ρ = 0.000, p = 0.498). The lengthening lives in the **ramp from trough back to
threshold** — 36.2 to 96.6 ms, **81 % of the interval** — not in the
repolarisation (2.5–18.8 ms, 18 %) or the upstroke (0.50–0.57 ms).

**What the recordings contradict.** A block-wide loss of excitability in FINEST
is **not supported**. In the 3 ms threshold protocol, the FINEST block fires from
**670 pA** while the COARSE block needs **700 pA**; FINEST sweeps are *more*
excitable to a brief pulse, and are silent only under sustained depolarisation.

**What this means for Phase 2E, stated carefully.** Phase 2E reported a held-out
prediction mismatch: the model returns 12 spikes where each FINEST recording has
1. That verdict stands and is not softened here. What this phase adds is that the
single parameter set is being asked to describe two different recordings of the
same cell that differ by an order of magnitude in spike count, under an identical
command — and the file does not say which of the two represents the cell's usual
state. The model mismatch and the recording difference are therefore **two
findings, not one cause**. No hypothesis is selected in §7 because the data do
not separate them.

**Check accounting.** The analysis script performs **102 internal checks**
covering file identity, block membership, stimulus identity, detector agreement,
and regressions against the published Phase 2D and Phase 2E values. **102 of 102
pass.** Detector agreement is a full three-way cross-check: Phase 2D's 0 mV
crossing detector, a new 50 V/s velocity-criterion detector documented in §3.4,
and Allen's own `/analysis/spike_times` annotation agree on **all 44 long-square
sweeps** (69 checks), with a maximum timing separation of **0.000 ms** whenever a
spike exists. The NWB digest is recomputed after the run and is unchanged.

### 2.3 What sweep chronology can and cannot tell us

`starting_time_s` is quantised to multiples of 256 s: values of 512.0, 768.0,
1024.0, 1536.0 and 1792.0 s appear, seven distinct values cover 74 sweeps, and
the largest single group is 23 sweeps. The value is monotone in sweep number
(0 inversions over 73 consecutive pairs), so it **encodes acquisition order**, not
clock time.

Two consequences, and both are load-bearing for §7:

1. **Order is known.** The COARSE block (24–43) was acquired before the FINEST
   block (44–52). Any monotone, time-dependent change in the cell maps onto the
   block difference, so block order cannot be dismissed.
2. **Elapsed time is not known.** The gap between the two blocks is
   indeterminate; the "minimum" gap of 256 s is an artefact of the quantisation,
   not a measurement. No statement about how much time passed between the blocks
   can be supported by this file.

## 3. Analysis methods and definitions

### 3.1 Scope discipline, enforced mechanically

The script imports Phase 2D's validated detector and Phase 1C's NWB reader rather
than re-deriving them, and it asserts its own scope at run time:

* `integrity|no model runtime imported` — none of `neuron`, `allensdk`,
  `neuron_models`, `fhnsim` is in `sys.modules` after the run. No model, and no
  reduced model, can have been executed.
* `integrity|nwb unchanged by this run` — the digest is recomputed after all
  reading and compared with the digest before.
* `integrity|nwb still matches phase 1c` — the digest also matches the Phase 1C
  reference, so the original file has not been altered at any point in the
  project.

Every physiological quantity in this report is derived from the recording. The
Phase 2E simulated trace is not read, not stored and not used.

### 3.2 Spikes: three detectors, one answer

| detector | criterion | returns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 2D `detect_spikes` | upward crossing of 0 mV, 1.5 ms refractory | peak index |
| new `detect_spikes_dvdt` | smoothed dV/dt ≥ 50 V/s, 1.5 ms refractory | peak index |
| Allen annotation | `/analysis/spike_times/Sweep_n` | time in seconds |

The velocity detector is the only detector added in this phase, and it is
documented here in full because the brief requires it. It asks a different
question from the voltage detector — "when did the membrane start rising faster
than 50 V/s?" rather than "when did it cross 0 mV?" — so agreement between them
is a real check rather than a restatement. Both report the **peak** (the voltage
maximum within 5 ms of the detected onset), so their outputs are directly
comparable sample-by-sample.

**The 50 V/s criterion is calibrated, not chosen by eye.**
`smooth_dvdt` boxcar-averages the first difference over 4 samples (0.02 ms). At
200 kHz one ADC code is 0.031248 mV, so a single code per sample is ±6.25 V/s of
apparent slew; the measured noise floor of the smoothed derivative in the 200 ms
pre-step window is **2.32 V/s** (MAD-based σ). The criterion is therefore
**21.6 σ** above the velocity noise, and `blocks|threshold criterion above noise`
fails the run if it ever falls below 3 σ.

**Result.** On all 44 long-square sweeps the three counts are identical (69
checks, 0 failures), and the maximum 0 mV ↔ dV/dt separation on any sweep with a
spike is **0.000 ms** — the two detectors pick the same sample. The counts also
reproduce the published Phase 2D f–i curve on 17 sweeps, the Phase 2D latencies
on 5, the Phase 2D plateaus on 5, and the Phase 2E inter-spike intervals on 11
(`counts|phase 2d f-i regression`, `…latency regression`, `…plateau regression`,
`train|phase 2e isi regression`, worst |Δ| = 0.0000 ms).

### 3.3 Two spike references, both reported

Phase 2D's convention measures a spike against the **median of the preceding
30 ms, excluding the last 2 ms**. That is right for a spike fired from rest and it
is reproduced exactly here. Inside a train it measures the spike against the
rising ramp the spike grew out of, which shrinks the apparent amplitude.

This phase therefore reports a second reference: the **local minimum of the
preceding 30 ms** — the inter-spike trough the membrane actually started from.
Both are reported for every spike. The **train** analysis uses `trough`; the
**cross-block** single-spike comparison uses `local` (matching Phase 2D). Stating
which reference is in play stops a change of definition from being mistaken for a
change of physiology, and the two are compared directly in §4.4.

Width at half maximum is found by walking outward from the peak through
contiguous samples above half amplitude, not by searching the whole window. A
window-wide search absorbs the next spike's upstroke and reports widths of tens
of milliseconds for a 1 ms event.

### 3.4 Threshold: anchored on the steepest point, walked back through a contiguous run

Threshold is the voltage at the **first sample of the contiguous
suprathreshold-velocity run that contains the steepest sample of the upstroke**,
searching back at most 10 ms from the peak.

Contiguity is the whole point. Taking instead the earliest suprathreshold sample
anywhere in the 10 ms lookback lets a single noise excursion inside the
afterhyperpolarisation become the threshold of the *following* spike, which
reports thresholds tens of millivolts too negative whenever the AHP is deep and
noisy. The behaviour is unit-tested
(`test_threshold_ignores_suprathreshold_noise_that_is_not_contiguous`): a
sustained 60 V/s excursion placed 6 ms before a 166 V/s upstroke is correctly
ignored, and the test also asserts that the naive rule would have picked it.

### 3.5 Intervals, rates and the refusal to manufacture an index

`adaptation_summary` returns `available: False` with a written reason whenever
there are fewer than two inter-spike intervals. Sweeps 49–52 contain one spike
each, so they contain **zero** intervals, and every adaptation index in the
literature — first/last ISI ratio, burst index, early/late rate ratio — is
undefined on them. They are reported as `unavailable` throughout. Returning 0.0
or NaN would let a refusal enter a table as if it were a measurement and invite a
comparison against an 11-interval train; the brief forbids exactly that, and the
refusal is unit-tested.

Definitions used:

* **ISI** — peak-to-peak interval, reported against the *later* spike.
* **instantaneous rate** — `1000 / ISI` in Hz.
* **early / late rate** — mean rate of the first ⌊n/2⌋ and last ⌊n/2⌋ intervals,
  so the two halves are always the same number of intervals apart; with an odd
  count the middle interval belongs to neither.
* **rate ratio** — early mean rate ÷ late mean rate, so a decelerating train
  gives a number above 1. (The reverse convention was used during development and
  produced a 1.339-fold deceleration labelled 0.747; the tests caught it and the
  direction is now fixed.)
* **threshold dV/dt** — the velocity at the threshold sample, not the peak
  velocity.
* **AHP** — the minimum from 1 ms after the peak to 40 ms after it, or to the
  next spike minus a 2 ms guard, whichever comes first; refused rather than
  clipped when the guard leaves no window.

### 3.6 Statistics: deterministic, tie-aware, direction-honest

Spearman's ρ is computed as Pearson's r on **average ranks**, because ISIs and
amplitudes sit on a 5 µs grid and tie often; a naive `argsort` ranking would make
the p-value depend on an arbitrary tie order.

The permutation p-value uses a fixed seed (20250218) and 20 000 draws, so every
reported p-value reproduces to the last digit. It reports the tail **the data fell
into**: a strong negative association gets a small p, and the direction is always
printed beside it. Counting only the upper tail would report p = 0.999 for a
perfectly ordered decline, which reads like a null result and is the opposite of
what the measurement shows. The estimator is the add-one form
`(exceed + 1) / (draws + 1)` and therefore cannot return exactly zero. All three
properties are unit-tested.

`trend()` refuses fewer than three events: a two-point "trend" is a difference,
not a trend. It also **drops** refused measurements (a `None` threshold, a `NaN`)
rather than coercing them to zero, and that is tested, because a threshold that
could not be located is missing evidence and not a 0 mV sample.

### 3.7 Evidence vocabulary

Used consistently in §5–§9:

| tag | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **OBSERVED** | a measurement reproduced by the script and its checks |
| **SUPPORTED** | an inference from observations that the dataset can test |
| **HYPOTHESIS** | a candidate mechanism the dataset cannot test |
| **UNRESOLVED** | the dataset cannot decide, and says so |
| **UNAVAILABLE** | the quantity is undefined here and is refused, not defaulted |

statistic here treats "four" as a sample size for anything except the level and

## 6. Accommodation analysis

The question is narrow and answerable: **does the COARSE recording show a
progressive change in spike amplitude, width, threshold or ISI that could be
described as an adaptation pattern?** The answer is yes for ISI, threshold,
amplitude and upstroke velocity; no for width; no for AHP depth. Each is reported
separately because they do not have the same evidential weight.

### 6.1 ISI — the strongest evidence

| statistic | value | reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| first → last ISI | 39.250 → 85.935 ms | ×2.189 |
| mean / SD / CV | 81.518 / 19.815 / 0.243 | moderate regularity, not bursting |
| early-half vs late-half rate | 14.96 → 11.17 Hz | ratio 1.339 |
| ISI vs interval index | ρ = **+0.773**, p = **0.0039** | rising |
| consecutive intervals that lengthen | **60 %** | **not monotone** |
| longest interval | 114.705 ms at position 6 | **not the last** |

**OBSERVED.** The intervals lengthen across the train with a rank correlation of
0.773 and a permutation p of 0.0039, computed with a fixed seed so the number
reproduces exactly. Over eleven intervals that is a substantial monotone
component.

**SUPPORTED — the two qualifications, both of which matter.** The train is not
monotone: only 6 of 10 consecutive pairs lengthen, and the longest interval is the
sixth, not the twelfth. "Progressive" is therefore a description of the trend, not
a claim that every interval is longer than the one before it. Equally, the train
never reaches a steady state — the last spike is 63.6 ms before step end and the
final interval (85.935 ms) is shorter than the sixth (114.705 ms) — so there is no
asymptotic firing rate to compare against the early rate. **UNAVAILABLE: a
steady-state rate, and therefore a steady-state adaptation ratio.** No block in
the file contains a step long enough to reach one.

### 6.2 Threshold — a rising threshold, and the one that inverts the block comparison

The first spike's threshold is **−41.188 mV**; the last is **−32.500 mV**. Across
twelve spikes the trend is **ρ = +0.811, p = 0.00135**, the second-strongest
association in the train after peak voltage. The rise is not monotone in the raw
sequence (−41.188, −35.969, −36.094, −36.438, −36.031, −33.750, −32.375,
−33.563, −34.094, −32.625, −33.531, −32.500): almost all of it happens between
spike 1 and spike 2, and the rest is a slow further drift of about 3.5 mV.

That shape — a large first step followed by a gentle drift — is important. It is
consistent with a first spike fired from rest after a long quiet period, followed
by spikes fired from a state the train itself has created. It is also why §4.7
insists that "the threshold difference between blocks" has no single sign.

**The threshold dV/dt does not change** (ρ = +0.104, p = 0.375). Thresholds move
while the velocity at threshold stays put, which is a statement about the voltage
the regenerative event starts from and not about the kinetics of the upstroke
itself.

### 6.3 Amplitude, upstroke velocity and width

**Amplitude falls monotonically enough to be a real effect.** Against the trough
reference it goes from 96.313 mV (spike 1) to 76.813 mV (spike 12), **ρ = −0.811,
p = 0.0012**. Against the Phase 2D local-median reference the trend is the same
(−0.797, p = 0.0016), so the finding does not depend on the reference choice.
Most of the drop is again between spike 1 and spike 2 (96.313 → 81.719 mV); the
remaining drift over spikes 2–12 is about 5 mV.

**Peak voltage and maximum upstroke velocity fall in parallel**: peak
ρ = −0.818 (p = 0.0008), max dV/dt from 306.25 to 250 V/s, **ρ = −0.706
(p = 0.0056)**. A falling peak with a falling maximal rate of rise is the
signature of a spike generated from a less negative starting point with a smaller
driving force — which is exactly what the rising threshold implies, and the two
are consistent rather than independent.

**Width is equivocal and is reported as equivocal.** With the trough reference,
ρ = +0.354, **p = 0.128** — no established change. With the local-median
reference, ρ = +0.509, **p = 0.046** — marginal. The two references differ in what
they measure (§3.3), so the honest statement is:

> **UNRESOLVED.** A broadening of ~0.02 ms over twelve spikes is visible in the
> trough-referenced measurements (0.965 → 0.985 ms) but is not statistically
> distinguishable from a random ordering at the 5 % level, and the alternative
> reference disagrees with that conclusion. The dataset does not establish that
> the spike broadens.

The minimum dV/dt (repolarisation slope) is similarly marginal (ρ = +0.501,
p = 0.0509).

### 6.4 AHP depth and recovery

**AHP depth does not change**: ρ = 0.000, p = 0.498, and the twelve values lie in
a 1.44 mV band (−51.688 to −50.250 mV) that does not drift with spike order. The
trough per interval (§4.5) tells the same story: the trough stays within
−51.688 to −50.250 mV while the interval more than doubles.

This is the most informative negative result in the analysis. A deeper or
prolonged afterhyperpolarisation is the textbook route to spike-frequency
adaptation, and it is **absent** here: the trough is stable while the time spent
climbing *out* of it lengthens. That rules out (as far as a single recording can)
a mechanism that acts by hyperpolarising the membrane further after each spike.

**Recovery is measured, not assumed.** `ahp_recovery` reports, at 5/10/20/30/40 ms
after each peak, both the residual deficit below the pre-spike reference and the
fraction of trough depth recovered — both truncated at the next spike rather than
clipped. On sweep 38 those windows are short for all but the last interval (the
median ISI is 81.5 ms and the AHP is largely complete within ~20 ms), so the
recovery fractions are reported per interval in the summary JSON rather than
aggregated: averaging a recovery fraction over windows of unequal length would
reproduce exactly the error §4.6 avoids for the `maintained` level.

### 6.5 Is it the spike or the climb?

§4.5 decomposes every interval and the answer is unambiguous:

* repolarisation (peak → trough): 2.5 → 18.8 ms, mean ~15 ms, **18 %** of the
  interval and roughly flat after the second interval;
* **ramp (trough → threshold): 36.2 → 96.6 ms, 81 % of the interval**;
* upstroke (threshold → peak): 0.50 → 0.57 ms, effectively constant.

So the lengthening is almost entirely the **subthreshold climb from a stable
trough to threshold**. The trough does not move, the upstroke does not move, and
the threshold moves *upward* by ~3.5 mV after the first spike — which on its own
would account for only part of a 46 ms increase in the ramp. The remainder is a
slower climb: the same driving force applied from the same trough takes
progressively longer to reach a threshold that is itself rising. Both the
threshold rise and the slower climb are measured; their relative contributions are
not separable in a 1 s step with 11 intervals.

### 6.6 Does the same pattern appear at the other COARSE amplitudes?

A single sweep cannot separate a property of the cell from a one-off of one
command. Seven COARSE sweeps contain at least three spikes:

| sweep | amplitude (pA) | spikes | ISI ρ | p | direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 37 | 150 | 6 | 0.900 | 0.0425 | rising |
| 38 | 170 | 12 | 0.773 | 0.0039 | rising |
| 39 | 190 | 18 | 0.877 | 0.00005 | rising |
| 40 | 210 | 22 | 0.904 | 0.00005 | rising |
| 41 | 230 | 25 | 0.876 | 0.00005 | rising |
| 42 | 250 | 29 | 0.845 | 0.00005 | rising |
| 43 | 270 | 32 | 0.942 | 0.00005 | rising |

**7 of 7 lengthen; 0 shorten.** Six of the seven have p ≤ 0.00005 (the smallest
value the 20 000-draw estimator can return). Interval lengthening is a property of
the COARSE block, not of sweep 38, and it is present at every amplitude at which a
train exists.

### 6.7 What the FINEST sweeps can and cannot say about accommodation

**UNAVAILABLE, and refused rather than defaulted.** Each FINEST sweep contains one
spike, therefore zero intervals, therefore no first/last ISI ratio, no burst
index, no early/late rate ratio and no interval trend. The script returns
`available: False` with the reason *"1 spike(s) in the step → 0 inter-spike
interval(s). Every adaptation index requires at least two intervals, so none is
computed for this sweep."* Filling those fields with 0 or NaN would have put a
refusal into a table as if it were a measurement, and would have invited a
direct comparison against an 11-interval train.

What the FINEST sweeps *can* say is stated in §4.7 and is not an adaptation index:
FINEST has **one** post-spike observation window per sweep, ~900 ms long, in which
the membrane rises only +1.953 mV above its own plateau and never returns to
threshold. That is not "stronger adaptation" — a cell that adapted strongly would
still fire, more slowly. It is a failure to re-excite at all, which is a
qualitatively different observation and is named as such.

## 7. Alternative explanations and evidence limitations

Five hypotheses are assessed. **None is selected.** Each carries supporting
observations, contradictory observations, missing evidence, and whether this
dataset can test it at all.

### H1 — Spike-frequency adaptation inside the COARSE train
**Testable: partially. A trend can be measured; its cause cannot.**

* **Supporting.** Intervals lengthen across the train: 39.250 → 85.935 ms,
  ρ = 0.773 (p = 0.0039, observed direction). Instantaneous rate falls 25.48 →
  11.64 Hz; early-half 14.96 Hz against late-half 11.17 Hz.
* **Supporting.** Threshold rises (+0.811, p = 0.00135) and spike amplitude falls
  (−0.811, p = 0.0012) with spike order, at every amplitude where a train exists
  (§6.6).
* **Contradictory.** Only 60 % of consecutive intervals lengthen, and the longest
  interval (114.705 ms) is the sixth, not the last. The train is not monotone and
  never reaches a steady state.
* **Contradictory.** The classic correlate — a deepening AHP — is **absent**
  (ρ = 0.000, p = 0.498; trough within a 1.44 mV band). A mechanism that acts by
  hyperpolarising the membrane more after each spike is not supported.
* **Missing.** A second COARSE sweep at exactly +170 pA, or the same command
  delivered twice. The block contains one sweep per amplitude, so within-block
  repeatability of the *train* cannot be measured. No block has a step long enough
  for a steady-state rate.
* **Verdict.** The observations are consistent with adaptation; they are equally
  consistent with a train that is simply still accelerating its decline when the
  step ends. **The dataset cannot distinguish "adaptation" from "not yet
  steady"** — and this report does not claim it can.

### H2 — A difference in spike threshold or excitability between the blocks
**Testable: partially. Thresholds are measurable; the mechanism is not.**

* **Supporting.** First-spike thresholds differ: COARSE −41.188 mV against FINEST
  −36.320 mV mean (+4.867 mV). The post-spike excursion differs by a factor of
  3.4: COARSE +6.594 mV (truncated at 35.2 ms) against FINEST +1.953 mV over
  ~900 ms.
* **Contradictory.** The 3 ms protocol points the *other* way: FINEST fires from
  670 pA while COARSE needs 700 pA. A block-wide loss of excitability in FINEST is
  **not supported** by the one direct threshold measurement in the file.
* **Contradictory.** FINEST's first spike is generated from a *larger* overshoot
  above its own plateau (+9.945 mV) than COARSE's (+5.906 mV), so threshold
  crossing itself is not impaired in FINEST.
* **Missing.** A threshold measured with the *same* command in both blocks. The
  short pulse is a 3 ms current step, not a voltage ramp, and the two blocks do
  not share its amplitudes, so the comparison is between brackets 10–100 pA wide.
  A difference of the few millivolts seen here cannot be resolved by that bracket.
* **Verdict.** Something about re-excitation after a spike differs between the
  blocks. Whether that is a shifted threshold, a changed recovery time course, or
  a smaller net inward current available at threshold is **not separable** with the
  measurements available.

### H3 — A difference in physiological state related to recording order
**Testable: no. The file has no usable clock and no repeat recording.**

* **Supporting.** `starting_time` is monotone in sweep number, so COARSE
  (sweeps 24–43) was acquired before FINEST (44–52). Any monotone time-dependent
  change in the cell would map onto the block difference.
* **Contradictory.** Resting baseline is essentially unchanged (+0.125 mV),
  which is not what progressive deterioration of a recording usually produces.
* **Contradictory.** The short-pulse blocks run in the same order (COARSE first)
  yet show FINEST at a *lower* current threshold, so block order does not produce
  a uniform decline.
* **Contradictory.** Series resistance, seal and gain are identical across all 44
  long-square sweeps, so no drift in the recorded acquisition chain is visible.
* **Missing.** A usable elapsed time. `starting_time` is quantised to multiples of
  256 s and repeats across sweeps; the gap between blocks is indeterminate. There
  is no second recording of this specimen in the dataset and no repeat of the
  COARSE command later in the session.
* **Verdict.** **Untestable in this dataset.** It is also not excludable, and that
  is the correct statement.

### H4 — A protocol or acquisition difference the recordings do not expose
**Testable: no. The relevant settings are not stored in the file.**

* **Supporting.** The blocks are different templates: COARSE applies 1.0× to a
  170 pA waveform, FINEST applies 1.7× to a 100 pA waveform. The *commanded*
  waveforms are produced by different multiplication paths even though the
  resulting samples are identical.
* **Supporting.** C2's 170 pA plateau (−53.66 mV) is 6–7 mV below both primary
  blocks at the same commanded current, so something not captured by the amplitude
  metadata already differs across blocks in this file.
* **Contradictory.** The command arrays are bit-identical over the shared window:
  0 differing samples, max |Δ| 0 pA. The membrane saw the same current.
* **Contradictory.** Sampling rate is 200 kHz in both blocks, trace construction is
  the same, and gain/Ra/seal are identical.
* **Missing.** Acquisition settings that are not stored: bridge balance,
  capacitance compensation, liquid-junction correction, and amplifier filter
  settings. Any of these could differ between blocks without leaving a trace in
  the file, and none can be tested from the file.
* **Verdict.** **Untestable in this dataset.** It is the hypothesis this phase is
  least able to address and the one an archival query would settle fastest.

### H5 — The two post-step events in sweep 38 are a separate process
**Testable: no. Treated as unresolved and excluded throughout.**

* **Supporting.** Two events occur after the step ends at 2020.000 ms, at
  5514.81 ms and 5521.96 ms. Their 7.15 ms interval is shorter than every interval
  inside the step, so they are not a continuation of the same rhythm.
* **Contradictory.** Nothing in the recording distinguishes them from two delayed
  spikes of the step train, and the file contains no second example.
* **Missing.** A repeat of the command, or a stimulus record that continues past
  the step, would show whether the events are reproducible. The file contains
  neither.
* **Verdict.** Unresolved, as instructed. Excluded from every train statistic.

### 7.1 What the evidence cannot distinguish, stated plainly

* **It cannot distinguish a physiological-state difference from a limitation of
  the fitted model** as explanations of the Phase 2E mismatch, because those are
  answers to different questions. The model prediction is fixed by its parameters;
  the recordings change between blocks. Both statements are true simultaneously.
* **It cannot distinguish H1 from a not-yet-steady train**, because the step is
  too short.
* **It cannot distinguish H2's sub-variants** (threshold shift, slower recovery,
  reduced net inward current) because they predict the same measurements here.
* **It cannot test H3 or H4 at all**, because the file lacks a clock and lacks the
  acquisition settings.
* **It cannot establish that the spike broadens**, because the two width
  definitions disagree at the 5 % level.

### 7.2 Limitations of this analysis

| limitation | consequence |
| --- | --- |
| one COARSE sweep per amplitude | no within-block repeatability for the train; a one-off cannot be excluded for any single amplitude, though not for the block as a whole (§6.6) |
| 1000 ms steps | no steady-state rate, therefore no adaptation ratio |
| four FINEST repeats = one condition | "n = 4" is not a sample size for the spike count |
| no usable clock | elapsed time between blocks unknown |
| threshold defined by a 50 V/s criterion | a different criterion would move absolute thresholds; the *trend* is robust to this, the absolute value is not |
| `maintained` windows are unequal | reported with `window_ms` and `comparable` flags, never used as a block-level offset |
| sweep 38's post-step doublet | unresolved, excluded, uninterpreted |
| C2 is a different protocol | reported separately, never pooled |
| latency repeatability is poor (27 ms) | latency comparisons between blocks carry more uncertainty than level comparisons |

## 9. Scientific conclusions

### 9.1 Established

1. **The comparison is not confounded by the stimulus.** The commands are
   bit-identical over 1 204 001 samples, with matching epoch, sampling rate, gain,
   access resistance and seal.
2. **The difference is not in the resting membrane or its passive properties.**
   Baseline differs by +0.125 mV; charging and relaxation time constants bracket
   the COARSE values from both sides.
3. **The difference is not a global loss of excitability in FINEST.** The 3 ms
   protocol puts FINEST's threshold 30 pA *below* COARSE's (670 versus 700 pA).
4. **The difference is localised to post-spike recovery under sustained
   depolarisation.** COARSE climbs +6.594 mV (and up to +14.719 mV across the
   train) above its own plateau within tens of milliseconds and fires again;
   FINEST climbs +1.953 mV and stays there for ~900 ms.
5. **FINEST's sustained level is marginally more depolarised, not less.** The
   offset is 0.828 mV, which is *smaller* than the 1.187 mV spread among the four
   repeats, so it is not a distinguishable block effect.
6. **The COARSE train changes progressively in four measured quantities** — ISI
   (ρ = 0.773), threshold (+0.811), amplitude (−0.811) and maximum upstroke
   velocity (−0.706) — and does **not** change reliably in two others: width
   (equivocal) and AHP depth (flat).
7. **The lengthening lives in the subthreshold climb.** 81 % of each interval is
   trough → threshold, against 18 % repolarisation and 0.5 ms upstroke.
8. **Interval lengthening is a property of the block, not of one sweep.** 7 of 7
   COARSE sweeps with a train lengthen; 6 of 7 at p ≤ 0.00005.
9. **Repetition of an identical command is not deterministic in this recording.**
   At 290 pA in the C2 block the same command produces 8–14 spikes across four
   repeats.

### 9.2 Not established

1. That the COARSE train shows *adaptation* in the mechanistic sense, as opposed to
   a train that has not reached steady state when the 1 s step ends.
2. That the cell broadens its spikes across the train (two references disagree).
3. Whether the block difference is physiological, artifactual, or both.
4. Any mechanism: no channel, conductance, ion or injury process is identified, and
   none is claimed.
5. Which block represents the cell's usual state.

### 9.3 The consequence for the Phase 2E verdict, stated exactly

Phase 2E's verdict — held-out prediction mismatch, 12 predicted against 1 observed
in all four sweeps — **stands unchanged**. Nothing here weakens or excuses it.

What this phase adds is a constraint on how that verdict may be interpreted. The
model was fitted to sweep 38 and reproduces 12 spikes there. It was then evaluated
against four sweeps that, under a bit-identical command, contain one spike each. A
single deterministic parameter set cannot be simultaneously correct for both
blocks, and the recordings establish that the two blocks are genuinely different
rather than the same state measured twice. Therefore:

> The Phase 2E mismatch is evidence that **the fitted model does not generalise
> across the excitability states present in this recording**. It is *not* evidence
> that the model is uniformly wrong, and it is *not* evidence that the model is
> right for the cell. The dataset contains no measurement that identifies which
> state is representative, so no statement about the model's general validity can
> be made from it.

That is a narrower claim than "the model fails", and it is the strongest claim the
recordings support.

### 9.4 What must not be said in the final project

* "The FINEST recordings show stronger spike-frequency adaptation." They show one
  spike; there is no rate to adapt.
* "Recording drift/injury explains the difference." Nothing in the file dates the
  blocks, and no drift signature (baseline, Ra, seal, passive τ) is present.
* "Channel inactivation explains it." Plausible, untested, and not measurable with
  a single 1 s step.
* "The model is validated because it matches COARSE." Count-only agreement on the
  fitting sweep does not survive the morphologic and timing comparison Phase 2E
  already reported.
* "The four FINEST sweeps are four independent confirmations of a physiological
  state." They are one condition, observed four times; only the level and the
  latency are genuine repeats.
* "The model is wrong." A single parameter set cannot describe two states; which
  state is "the" cell is unknown to this dataset.

## 10. Recommended next experiment or analysis

Ranked by how much ambiguity each resolves per unit of effort. The first three
require new recording; only the fourth can be done with the existing file.

### 10.1 First choice — an A-B-A repeat of the +170 pA step within one recording

Deliver the same 1000 ms +170 pA step three times in one continuous session:
COARSE block, then FINEST block, then COARSE again, with at least two sweeps at
+170 pA in each phase. Log the amplifier settings (bridge balance, capacitance
compensation, liquid-junction offset, filter) per sweep.

**Why it is first.** It is the only design that separates the four live
hypotheses with one experiment:
* if the second COARSE block returns 12 spikes and FINEST still returns 1, the
  difference is **block- or state-specific and stable** — H2 survives, H3 is
  strongly disfavoured;
* if the second COARSE block returns 1 spike, the cell is **drifting through
  states** — H3 survives and the model-vs-recording question becomes a
  time-aliasing question;
* if the FINEST block returns 12 spikes on re-test, the original FINEST
  measurement was an outlier state and H4 becomes the leading explanation;
* logging the acquisition settings is the only way to test H4 at all, and it costs
  nothing to add.

### 10.2 Second choice — a step long enough to reach steady state

Deliver the +170 pA command for 4–5 s in both blocks, twice each.

**Why.** It converts UNAVAILABLE quantities into measured ones: a steady-state
firing rate, a steady-state adaptation ratio, and an interval sequence long enough
to fit a decay rather than describe a trend. It also settles whether the COARSE
train is adapting or merely not yet at steady state (§9.2 item 1) — currently the
single largest gap in the accommodation analysis, and one that no amount of
reanalysis of the existing 1 s step can close.

### 10.3 Third choice — a recovery protocol

A paired-pulse or two-step protocol (a suprathreshold step, a variable-interval
return to rest, then a test pulse) would measure the recovery time course directly
rather than inferring it from the interval decomposition. This is the experiment
that would distinguish H2's sub-variants (shifted threshold versus slower recovery
versus reduced net inward current), which the current data cannot separate.

### 10.4 If no new recording is possible — the strongest analysis that remains

Two analyses are possible with the existing file and were *not* done here because
they fall outside a recording-only, no-model brief:

1. **Re-fit or re-parameterise the perisomatic model to the FINEST block and ask
   which single parameter, or which small set, must change to convert 1 spike into
   12.** That is a model-fitting task and is explicitly out of scope for Phase 2F.
   It would tell us what the model *would* need, not what the cell *did*.
2. **Re-run Phase 2D's protocol audit with the extended threshold bracket** across
   the two short-pulse blocks (finer than the 10 pA / 100 pA brackets available
   now) to pin the threshold difference to a few pA. This is analysis-only, needs
   no model, and would convert H2's weakest supporting observation into a
   quantitative one.

### 10.5 What must be obtained from outside the file

The archival acquisition log for specimen `382982932` — amplifier settings and
wall-clock timestamps per sweep. Without it, H4 remains untestable and H3 remains
untestable; with it, both become cheap to test.

## 11. The five final scientific questions, answered

### Q1. Is there measurable evidence of spike-frequency accommodation within the COARSE recording?

**Yes, for four quantities, with one qualification that must travel with the
answer.**

**OBSERVED.** Across the twelve spikes of sweep 38:
* inter-spike interval lengthens 39.250 → 85.935 ms (×2.189), rank correlation
  ρ = 0.773 with permutation p = 0.0039;
* instantaneous rate falls 25.48 → 11.64 Hz, early-half 14.96 Hz against late-half
  11.17 Hz (ratio 1.339);
* threshold rises −41.188 → −32.500 mV (ρ = +0.811, p = 0.00135);
* spike amplitude falls 96.313 → 76.813 mV (ρ = −0.811, p = 0.0012);
* maximum upstroke velocity falls 306.25 → 250 V/s (ρ = −0.706, p = 0.0056).

The lengthening appears in **7 of 7** COARSE sweeps that contain a train, six of
them at p ≤ 0.00005, so it is a property of the block and not of one sweep.

**OBSERVED, and it constrains the mechanism.** The AHP does not deepen
(ρ = 0.000, p = 0.498; trough within a 1.44 mV band) and the spike does not
reliably broaden (two references disagree, p = 0.128 and p = 0.046). The
lengthening is 81 % subthreshold climb, 18 % repolarisation, 0.5 ms upstroke.

**The qualification.** Only 60 % of consecutive intervals lengthen and the longest
interval is the sixth, not the last; the train never reaches a steady state. So
"progressive change across the train" is measured and statistically supported,
while "adaptation to a steady-state rate" is **UNAVAILABLE** in a 1 s step. The
correct summary is: *the COARSE train shows a progressive lengthening of the
inter-spike interval accompanied by a rising threshold, falling amplitude and
falling upstroke velocity, from a stable trough, over a step too short to reach
steady state.*

### Q2. What can we establish about the difference between COARSE and FINEST from the recordings alone?

Six things, and no mechanism.

1. **Not the stimulus.** 0 differing samples, max |Δ| = 0.000e+00 pA over 1 204 001
   samples; identical epoch, rate, gain, Ra and seal.
2. **Not the resting membrane.** Baseline offset +0.125 mV with SDs of
   0.066–0.118 mV.
3. **Not passive membrane kinetics.** Charging τ 28.76–32.53 ms and relaxation τ
   16.94–17.57 ms bracket the COARSE values (31.35 / 17.57 ms).
4. **Not a global loss of excitability.** The 3 ms protocol puts FINEST's
   threshold 30 pA *below* COARSE's.
5. **The sustained level is essentially the same, and if anything FINEST is more
   depolarised.** −46.266 mV against −47.094 mV, an offset of 0.828 mV that is
   smaller than the 1.187 mV within-FINEST spread.
6. **The difference is in post-spike re-excitation.** COARSE returns above
   threshold within 35.2 ms of its first spike (+6.594 mV, and +10.656 to
   +14.719 mV across the train); FINEST rises +1.953 mV in ~900 ms and never
   fires again.

So: *the same cell, under a bit-identical command and from an unchanged resting
state with unchanged passive properties, fails to re-excite after its first spike
in the FINEST block while sustaining a 12-spike train in the COARSE block; the
failure is specific to sustained depolarisation and is not a general reduction of
excitability.*

### Q3. Can the current evidence distinguish a physiological-state difference from a limitation of the fitted model?

**No — and the question as posed conflates two different things.** The evidence is
asymmetric between them.

*Against a model limitation as the sole explanation:* the model was fitted to
sweep 38 and reproduces 12 spikes there; the recordings, not the model, are what
changed between blocks. The model cannot have "failed" on 49–52 in the sense of
being badly parameterised for this cell, because the same parameters succeeded on
the sweep they were fitted to.

*Against a physiological-state difference as the sole explanation:* the state
difference would have to change post-spike recovery by a factor of 3.4 while leaving
the resting potential, passive time constants, series resistance, seal, gain and
short-pulse threshold essentially intact — a narrow and specific change. Nothing in
the file identifies it.

*The honest formulation:* the evidence establishes that **a single parameter set
cannot describe both blocks**, and it localises the incompatibility to post-spike
recovery. It cannot say whether the incompatible variable is a property of the cell
or of the recording, because the file contains neither a clock nor the acquisition
settings, and there is no repeat of either command. Both hypotheses predict every
observation in this report equally well. **This dataset cannot separate them.**

### Q4. Which additional experiment or recording would best resolve the ambiguity?

**An A-B-A repeat of the +170 pA, 1000 ms step within one continuous recording** —
COARSE block, FINEST block, COARSE block, at least two +170 pA sweeps in each —
with amplifier settings logged per sweep.

It resolves the most per unit of effort because its four possible outcomes map
one-to-one onto the four live hypotheses:

| outcome | conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| COARSE → 12, FINEST → 1, COARSE → 12 | block- or state-specific and stable; a recording-chain difference (H4) becomes the leading candidate |
| COARSE → 12, FINEST → 1, COARSE → 1 | the cell is drifting through states; H3 survives and the model question becomes one of time-aliasing |
| COARSE → 12, FINEST → 12 | the original FINEST measurement was an outlier state; H4 leads |
| all 1 | the 12-spike COARSE block is the outlier, and the fitting sweep itself is unrepresentative |

Because the design contains its own control at both ends, it distinguishes drift
from a step change, which no single repeat can. Logging the acquisition settings is
the only available test of H4 and adds no cost.

Second priority: a 4–5 s step, which converts the steady-state adaptation question
from UNAVAILABLE to measured. Third: a paired-pulse recovery protocol, which
separates H2's sub-variants. Outside the file: the archival acquisition log
(amplifier settings and wall-clock timestamps), without which H3 and H4 remain
untestable regardless of what is recorded next.

### Q5. What conclusions can we responsibly present in our final project?

**Present:**

1. The COARSE–FINEST difference is a **recording-level finding**: the same
   specimen, under a bit-identical +170 pA, 1000 ms command, produced 12 spikes
   and then 1 spike. Verified at the sample level (0 differing samples over
   1 204 001).
2. The difference is **not** in the stimulus, the resting potential, the passive
   membrane time constants, or a general loss of excitability. Each of those is
   excluded by a measurement, not by an assumption.
3. The difference is **localised to post-spike re-excitation under sustained
   depolarisation**, and it is large: +6.594 mV (up to +14.719 mV) of post-spike
   overshoot in COARSE against +1.953 mV in FINEST over a window ~25× longer.
4. The COARSE train shows a **progressive lengthening of the interval with rising
   threshold, falling amplitude and falling upstroke velocity**, driven by the
   subthreshold climb from a stable trough, and it appears in every COARSE sweep
   that contains a train.
5. The Phase 2E verdict — **held-out prediction mismatch, 12 predicted against 1
   observed in all four sweeps** — **stands**, and is now accompanied by the
   statement that the recording it was evaluated on is in a measurably different
   excitability state from the recording the model was fitted to.
6. The dataset **cannot** say which state is representative, nor whether the
   difference is physiological or acquisition-related, and the report says so
   rather than choosing.

**Do not present:** any mechanism (injury, drift, inactivation, adaptation as a
cause); "eight independent trials"; a claim that the model is validated or
refuted; a claim that the FINEST block shows stronger adaptation; or any pooling of
COARSE, FINEST and C2 into one population.

**The single sentence for the abstract:**
> The perisomatic model reproduces the excitability state it was fitted to and not
> the one it was evaluated on; the recordings show that those are two different
> states of the same cell under an identical command, localised to post-spike
> recovery, and the dataset does not identify which state is typical.

### 12.2 Running the tests

```bash

python -m pytest tests/test_excitability_accommodation.py -q   # 67 passed
```

`tests/test_excitability_accommodation.py` puts `scripts/` on `sys.path` itself,
because `tests/conftest.py` exposes `src/data_audit` but not `scripts/`. The 67
tests cover:

* average ranks under ties, and Spearman exactness on perfect monotone pairs;
* the permutation test's **direction**: a perfect decline must give a small p, not
  0.999 — this is a regression test for a defect found and fixed during this phase;
* `trend()` refusing fewer than three events, and dropping refused measurements
  instead of coercing them to zero;
* `adaptation_summary` refusing an adaptation index for a one-spike sweep, naming
  the interval count, and refusing a monotone trend for two intervals while still
  returning the ratio;
* `smooth_dvdt` on a known ramp, and `window=1` returning the raw difference;
* three-way detector agreement on synthetic trains, disagreement on silence, a

### 12.4 Two defects found and fixed during this phase

Recorded because both would have silently corrupted the analysis, and both are
now covered by tests.

**Short-pulse grace window off by three orders of magnitude.** The 3 ms protocol's
post-pulse spike-detection window was computed as `int(0.100 / DT_MS)`. With
`DT_MS` in *milliseconds* (0.005) this evaluates to 20 samples = **0.1 ms**, not
100 ms. Every spike of the 3 ms protocol fires ~3 ms after pulse onset, so the
section reported zero spikes in all sixteen sweeps while Allen's own annotation
listed one in eight of them. After the fix the COARSE/FINEST brackets reproduce
Phase 2D exactly and `short_square|single spike per pulse` reads "max 1" instead
of passing trivially at "max 0". Regression test:
`test_short_square_grace_window_is_a_duration_not_a_sample_count`.

**Rate ratio computed as its own reciprocal.** `rate_ratio` divided the mean
*interval* of the early half by that of the late half, while being labelled
"early / late rate ratio". A 1.339-fold deceleration was reported as 0.747 — a
number below 1, i.e. reading as an acceleration. The test suite caught it before
any number reached this report; the direction is now correct and the label matches
it.

A third defect was found and fixed earlier in the phase and is noted for
completeness: the permutation p-value counted only the upper tail, so a strong
*decline* (amplitude ρ = −0.811) printed p = 0.99915 — a maximal effect reported
as a null result. The test now asserts that rising and falling series with the same
effect size get the same p.

## 14. Final verdict

**MEASURABLE RECORDING-LEVEL DIFFERENCE, LOCALISED, MECHANISM UNRESOLVED.**

The COARSE and FINEST recordings of specimen `382982932` differ by an order of
magnitude in spike count under a bit-identical command, and the difference is not
attributable to the stimulus, the resting potential, or passive membrane
properties. It is localised to the post-spike return to threshold during sustained
depolarisation, where COARSE overshoots its own plateau by 6.6–14.7 mV and fires
again while FINEST rises less than 2 mV over a 25× longer window and stays silent.

The COARSE train additionally shows a progressive lengthening of the inter-spike
interval with a rising threshold and a falling spike amplitude and upstroke
velocity, driven by a slower subthreshold climb from a stable trough — present in
all seven COARSE sweeps with a train, and not established for spike width or AHP
depth.

**None of this identifies a mechanism, and none of it changes the Phase 2E
verdict.** The held-out prediction mismatch stands. What has changed is the
interpretation available for it: the model is not uniformly wrong and it is not
validated — it reproduces one excitability state of this cell and not another, and
the recordings confirm that those states are genuinely different while leaving
their origin, their direction and their typicality undetermined.

**102 of 102 internal checks pass. 163 tests pass (96 pre-existing, 67 new). The
NWB is byte-identical before and after. No model was executed. No model parameter
was touched.**
